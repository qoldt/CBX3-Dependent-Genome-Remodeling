# The large one-directional blocks: do they move contact, and in what chromatin?

Written 2026-10-20. Every number here is in `results/tables/`; re-derive rather
than re-quote. Regenerated by `GAM_4_Large_Scale.R` (blocks, budget) plus the
two block-class tables described at the bottom.

**What a block is.** `GAM_4` smooths `net = log2((gained + 1) / (lost + 1))` over
a 1 Mb running mean within each chromosome, where gained/lost are counts of
significant differential contacts (|delta-z| > 2, NPMI > 0.1, within 0-5 Mb)
carried by each 20 kb bin. A block is a run where the smoothed net stays beyond
0.3 in absolute value for at least 4 Mb. There are **123**: 63 lost, 60 gained,
covering 1,025 Mb (lost 554 Mb, gained 471 Mb).

## 1. Lost and gained blocks are the same size

| | lost | gained |
|---|---|---|
| blocks | 63 | 60 |
| median width | 7.64 Mb | 7.40 Mb |
| max width | **25.84 Mb** | 15.79 Mb |
| total | 554 Mb | 471 Mb |

Wilcoxon on width **p = 0.465**. The medians are indistinguishable; the only
asymmetry is the tail, where the largest lost block is 25.8 Mb against 15.8 Mb
for the largest gained one. **A reader expecting lost blocks to be visibly
larger in the region plots will not find it, and should not go looking** - the
block-window figures are picked by rank, so a 10 Mb lost block beside a 10 Mb
gained block is the typical case, not a coincidence.

## 2. Blocks do move contact, and far harder than the genome average

`gam8_block_contact_budget.tsv`, mean NPMI change by where each end of the pair
sits. Rows are strictly ordered at every separation:

| both ends in | <1 Mb | 1-5 Mb | 5-10 Mb | 10-50 Mb | >50 Mb |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| gained block, both | **+4.41%** | **+14.49%** | +13.08% | +6.46% | +4.94% |
| gained block + elsewhere | -1.23% | +2.74% | +3.78% | -1.45% | +3.74% |
| neither (background) | -0.74% | -3.97% | -5.82% | -8.33% | -2.84% |
| lost block + elsewhere | -0.60% | -8.98% | -15.78% | -16.80% | -15.68% |
| lost block, both | **-4.93%** | **-18.68%** | **-25.25%** | -23.57% | -22.83% |
| lost block to gained block | -10.98% | -0.75% | -5.94% | -12.00% | -9.43% |

Two things to take from it. The ordering gained-gained > gained-other >
background > lost-other > lost-lost holds in **every** band, and the
within-block magnitudes (-25%, +14%) are two to three times the genome-wide
chromatin-class effects they sit inside (`gam10_class_change.tsv`, -11% to
+1.6%). **The blocks are where most of the contact change lives.**

**This is agreement, not independent confirmation.** Blocks are defined from
thresholded counts of significant differential contacts; the table above is a
mean of continuous NPMI. The two are different statistics, but computed from the
same contact matrices, so a shared artefact would move both. What the table adds
is magnitude and distance dependence, which the block definition does not carry.

## 3. The contacts inside blocks are mostly quiescent, and that differs by direction

`gam16_block_contact_classes.tsv` - both ends classified on the level-2
chromatin partition, both ends required to be in the *same* block.

Composition of within-block pairs:

| class pair | lost blocks | gained blocks |
|---|---|---|
| Quiescent-Quiescent | **57.4%** | 47.9% |
| Tx-Quiescent | 23.7% | 27.0% |
| H3K9me3-Quiescent | 9.3% | 9.3% |
| Tx-Tx | 4.0% | 5.6% |
| Tx-H3K9me3 | 1.9% | 3.1% |
| **H3K9me3-H3K9me3** | **0.57%** | **2.73%** |

H3K9me3-H3K9me3 pairs are **4.8x more common inside gained blocks than inside
lost ones**. That is a much sharper separation than the bins themselves give
(10.97% of gained-block bins are H3K9me3 against 7.04% of lost-block bins, a
1.6x ratio, with the genome's non-block bins at 9.31%) - because a pair needs
*both* ends in the class, so a bin-level enrichment is roughly squared, and
because H3K9me3 bins are more clustered inside gained blocks.

Both block sets are nonetheless dominated by quiescent chromatin. **Neither kind
of block is an H3K9me3 domain**; the user-inspected examples are typical, not
exceptions (chr4:23.8-30.4 lost = 8.2% H3K9me3 / 87.8% quiescent;
chr2:165.0-175.3 gained = 3.8% / 64.8%).

## 4. H3K9me3 pairs do better than quiescent pairs in the same block, either way

Percent change in mean NPMI, 95% CI bootstrapped over the 19 autosomes
(`gam16_block_class_ci.tsv`):

| | | H3K9me3-H3K9me3 | Quiescent-Quiescent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **lost** | <0.2 Mb | +0.34 [-0.57, +1.68] | -0.46 [-1.11, +0.23] |
| | 0.2-1 Mb | -4.12 [-5.13, -2.69] | -5.57 [-6.71, -4.32] |
| | 1-5 Mb | -10.72 [-16.38, -8.04] | -17.55 [-20.15, -15.31] |
| **gained** | <0.2 Mb | +6.70 [+5.33, +7.63] | +3.85 [+2.85, +4.77] |
| | 0.2-1 Mb | +10.13 [+5.67, +13.20] | +5.25 [+3.41, +6.87] |
| | 1-5 Mb | +14.65 [+7.78, +22.19] | +16.06 [+11.17, +20.49] |

The difference, bootstrapped directly on the same chromosome draw so a
block-level offset cancels (`gam16_block_class_contrast_ci.tsv`):

| H3K9me3-H3K9me3 minus Quiescent-Quiescent | <0.2 Mb | 0.2-1 Mb | 1-5 Mb | 5-10 Mb |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| inside lost blocks | +0.80 [-0.28, +2.40] | **+1.45 [+0.41, +2.81]** | **+6.84 [+2.81, +8.92]** | +5.37 [-3.98, +13.76] |
| inside gained blocks | **+2.85 [+1.39, +4.09]** | **+4.88 [+0.28, +8.32]** | -1.41 [-8.28, +6.34] | -2.51 [-11.39, +9.48] |

**The H3K9me3 short-range advantage is independent of which way the block is
going.** It clears zero at 0.2-1 Mb in both directions, and below 0.2 Mb in
gained blocks. Inside lost blocks, H3K9me3-H3K9me3 is the one class pair that
does not fall at short range at all (+0.34%, CI spanning 0) while everything
around it drops. So the genome-wide level-2 result - H3K9me3 gains short-range
contact - is not produced by the gained blocks: it survives *inside* blocks that
are losing contact overall.

The two effects are separable and of different sizes: block direction is worth
+14% to -25%, the H3K9me3-over-quiescent contrast within a block worth +1 to +5
percentage points.

## Caveats

- **The 10-50 Mb within-block band is not usable.** Only blocks wider than 10 Mb
  contribute, so it rests on 6-14 chromosomes instead of 19 and its intervals are
  three times wider. It is in the tables; do not quote it.
- **2 vs 2 biological replicates, already merged.** No per-contact p-value exists.
  Every interval here is a bootstrap over chromosomes, which is the only
  resampling unit the data supports.
- **Sections 3 and 4 use the level-2 ChromHMM partition**, not the old
  H3K9me3/H4K20me3 peak binary. Numbers from before 2026-10-19 are not comparable.
- `net` smoothing and the 0.3 cutoff are `gam_block_net_cut` / `gam_block_min_mb`
  in `config.R`; the block set changes if they do.
